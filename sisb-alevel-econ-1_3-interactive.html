Land, labour, capital or enterprise?

Land is natural resources — not just the ground, but minerals, forests, rivers, climate, sea and sun. Labour is human resources. Capital is anything made by humans that aids production. Enterprise organises the other three and takes the risk.

Task B — human capital or physical capital?
Physical capital is the machinery, buildings and infrastructure made by firms and governments. Human capital is the value of labour to the future productive potential of an economy — the skills, knowledge and experience embodied in people. Both are investments; only one of them can walk out of the door.

Exam tip the coursebook makes explicitly: the quality of a factor is often more important than its quantity. A country with a large workforce but little training may be poorer than one with a smaller, highly skilled one. Write about quality and you separate yourself from most of the cohort.

Activity 3 · Coursebook 3.3

Specialisation and the division of labour

Specialisation is individuals, firms, regions or whole economies concentrating on producing some goods rather than others. The division of labour is the version inside a workplace: splitting a production process into a sequence of separate tasks.

Adam Smith's pin factory made the point in 1776 — 18 separate operations raised output from a few dozen pins per worker per day to around 5,000. Ford's conveyor belt did the same for cars in the 1920s. Work through the numbers first, then weigh it up.

Exam tip: "the division of labour raises output" is an AO1 statement. "The division of labour raises output because workers repeat one task, become faster at it, and lose no time switching between tools — so output per worker rises and unit costs fall" is AO2. Why enterprise is treated as a separate factor

Labour is hired at an agreed wage — a contractual payment, owed whether or not the firm succeeds. Enterprise is different: the entrepreneur organises the other three factors and is paid only out of what is left over. That residual is profit, and it may be negative.

That is the whole reason enterprise gets its own category. It is not a type of work; it is the bearing of uninsurable risk. When Dyson abandoned his electric car after a very large investment, that loss fell on the entrepreneur — not on the engineers, who had already been paid.
