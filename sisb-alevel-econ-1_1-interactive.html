Activity 2 · AO1 + AO2 Application and analysis

Work out the opportunity cost

Opportunity cost is the benefit of the next best alternative forgone — one alternative, not all of them, and not the option that was actually chosen. Read each Thai scenario, pick your answer, then check the reasoning.

✓ All five scenarios attempted. Now write this out in one sentence: "The opportunity cost of X is Y, because Y was the highest-valued alternative given up when X was chosen." That sentence structure earns the definition mark in almost any 9708 question on this topic.

Three traps that cost marks every year

1. Adding up all the alternatives. If three options are given up, the opportunity cost is only the best one, not the sum.

2. Quoting the price of what was chosen. The ฿1,500 you spent is the money cost. The opportunity cost is what that ฿1,500 could have bought instead.

3. Forgetting non-money costs. Time, effort and forgone earnings are opportunity costs even when no money changes hands.

Activity 3 · Section 1.4 of the coursebook

What to produce, how to produce, for whom to produce?

Every economy must answer the same three questions, because scarcity forces resources to be allocated. Sort each Thai example into the question it is really answering.

What = which goods and services, and in what quantities. How = which combination of factors of production and which methods. For whom = how output is distributed between people.

Exam tip: a decision about which technique to use is always a "how" question, even when it changes who gets employed. The employment effect is a consequence — the decision itself is about method.

Activity 4 · AO3 Evaluation

Is the claim true, false, or does it depend?

AO3 is worth 25% of the qualification and 30% of Papers 2 and 4 — and it is where most AS candidates lose marks. Evaluation is not "on the other hand"; it is recognising the assumption a claim rests on and saying when the claim holds and when it does not.

Judge each statement, reveal the economist's view, then read the purple line — that shows you how the same point would be written in an essay.

✓ All five judged. Notice the pattern: nearly every strong evaluation point named an assumption, a timescale, or a group of people affected. Those three are your fastest route to AO3 marks under exam pressure.

Scarcity is universal; its severity is not

The fundamental economic problem applies to every economic agent in every economy, at every income level — a billionaire, a Bangkok street vendor, and the Royal Thai Government all face limited resources against unlimited wants.

But universality is not equality. The consequences of scarcity fall very unevenly, which is exactly why economics studies inequality, living standards and development rather than stopping at the abstract idea of choice. Saying both things in an essay — universal condition, unequal consequences — is worth more than saying either one alone.
