What you are actually being assessed on

Most lost marks are not lost through ignorance. They are lost because a candidate wrote a good answer to a slightly different question, or spent nine minutes on a four-mark part. Know the shape of the paper and you get those marks back for free.

The three assessment objectives

Across the whole qualification: AO1 Knowledge and understanding 35%, AO2 Analysis 40%, AO3 Evaluation 25%. Analysis is the largest single component — which surprises most students, who assume knowledge is.

AO1 · Knowledge
Recall facts, formulae and definitions. Explain with appropriate examples. Apply understanding to written, numerical and diagrammatic information.
AO2 · Analysis
Examine issues and relationships using economic concepts. Interpret and organise information. Recognise causes and effects, and explain the consequences of changes in economic variables.
AO3 · Evaluation
Recognise assumptions and limitations of models. Assess the strengths and weaknesses of arguments. Recognise priorities and value judgements. Communicate reasoned judgements and conclusions.

Timing: the arithmetic nobody does

Paper 2 and Paper 4 give you two hours for 60 marks — two minutes per mark, minus reading time. A 12-mark part deserves roughly 22 minutes and about a page and a half. A 4-mark part deserves about seven minutes and a short paragraph.

The single most common self-inflicted wound at AS is writing a beautiful 25-minute answer to a 4-mark part and then running out of time on a 12-mark evaluation. Marks are not awarded for effort; they are awarded per band, and no band above the maximum exists.

Build the chain of reasoning

Analysis is not description. Analysis is a chain: each step causes the next, and every link is stated rather than assumed. An examiner following your chain should never have to fill in a gap for you — because they will not.

Click the steps in the order they occur. Then check the chain and read the model paragraph, which shows the same chain written as continuous prose.

✓ All four chains built. The connective words that hold a chain together are: because, therefore, this means that, which leads to, so. If a paragraph of yours contains none of them, it is almost certainly description, not analysis.
AO3 Evaluation · 30% of Papers 2 and 4

What evaluation is, and what it is not

Evaluation is not "however, there are disadvantages". That is a signpost with nothing behind it, and it earns nothing. Evaluation is a reasoned judgement supported by economics: it names the assumption a claim rests on, or the condition under which it holds, or the group for whom it does not.

The conclusion that scores

A conclusion must answer the question that was set, not summarise your essay. If the question asks "to what extent", say to what extent. If it asks "assess whether", come down on one side.

And justify the judgement with something from your own analysis: "On balance the tax is likely to reduce consumption only modestly, because demand is price inelastic in the short run; it is therefore better justified as a revenue-raising measure than as a public health one — unless it is combined with policies that make substitutes more available."

Notice what that does: it takes a side, gives the economic reason, states the condition that would change the answer. Three sentences, and it is doing everything AO3 asks for.

The fastest three fixes

1. Define the key term in the question in your first two lines. Nearly free AO1 marks, and it forces you to answer the question asked.

2. Draw the diagram and refer to it in the prose. An unlabelled diagram floating beside an essay earns little; "as Figure 1 shows, supply shifts from S to S1 and price rises from P to P1" earns AO2.

3. Make the last paragraph a judgement, not a summary. If your final paragraph could be deleted without losing any information, it was a summary.
