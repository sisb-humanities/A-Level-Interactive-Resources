Activity 9.2, question 3 · How do the PES estimates relate to the shape of the supply curve? Explain why this might be so.
Your answer
Model answer. The PES rises as the price falls: 0 between $90 and $80, then about 1.26, 3.06 and 6.00. At the top the curve is vertical because the producer is already at full capacity, 19 sacks, so a higher price cannot draw out any more tea. Lower down the curve becomes much flatter, because at low prices tea is barely worth picking and small changes in price decide whether whole areas of the estate are harvested at all. At $50 the producer supplies nothing, which is the price below which it is not worth producing.
Part B · Rearranging the formula
Paper 1 often gives you the PES and asks for one of the percentage changes. If PES = %ΔQs ÷ %ΔP, then %ΔQs = PES × %ΔP and %ΔP = %ΔQs ÷ PES.

Activity 4 · Why PES matters

Same rise in demand, very different results

When demand increases, the extra buyers have to be met somehow: either producers make more, or the price rises until some of those buyers give up. PES decides the split. With inelastic supply most of the shift goes into a higher price; with elastic supply most of it goes into extra output.

Step 2 · The 8-mark answer
8
Explain why the price elasticity of supply of agricultural products is usually lower than that of manufactured goods. [8]
Plan before you write: define PES with the formula, then take the three factors one at a time and apply each to both kinds of product. A diagram with two supply curves through the same starting point earns marks of its own.
Your answer
Model answer. Price elasticity of supply (PES) measures the responsiveness of the quantity supplied to a change in the product’s price, and is calculated as the percentage change in quantity supplied divided by the percentage change in price. Supply is price inelastic when PES is less than 1. Agricultural supply is usually inelastic because of the time period: once a crop is planted, the size of the harvest is fixed for that season, and for tree crops such as durian or rubber a new planting takes years to produce anything. Many farm products are also perishable, so farmers cannot build up stocks and release them when the price rises. Manufactured goods are usually more elastic. A factory with spare capacity can add a shift or run its machines for longer within days, finished goods can be held in a warehouse and released straight away, and components can usually be bought from many suppliers. On a diagram, a steep supply curve for rice and a flatter one for electric fans drawn through the same starting point show that the same price rise brings a much smaller increase in quantity for rice. This matters because inelastic supply makes farm prices swing sharply whenever demand or the weather changes. The gap is not fixed, however: farm supply becomes more elastic in the long run, and a factory already at full capacity or short of skilled workers can face inelastic supply too.
Why this scores: it defines PES and gives the formula, applies each of the three coursebook factors to both kinds of product with a real example, uses a diagram, follows the point through to its consequence and adds a limitation. That is a complete chain of analysis rather than a list of facts.

Step 3 · The 12-mark answer
12
Assess whether a large rise in demand from China for Thai durian is likely to benefit Thai durian farmers more in the short run than in the long run. [12]
An assess question needs both sides and a supported judgement. Plan it here in note form, four or five bullet points, then compare with the model plan. Use the Demand Shifts lab to picture both time periods first.
Your plan
Model plan.
1 · Short run. Supply is highly price inelastic. The harvest is fixed by the trees already in the ground, a new tree takes four to five years to fruit, and fresh durian cannot be stored for long. A rightward shift in demand therefore goes almost entirely into price. Farmers with mature trees sell roughly the same quantity at a much higher price, so their revenue and profit rise sharply. Diagram: steep S, D shifts to D₁, large rise in P, small rise in Q.
2 · Long run. The high price is a signal. Existing farmers plant more trees and others switch land into durian, so supply becomes more elastic and, as new trees fruit, supply shifts right. Price falls back towards cost and quantity is far higher. The industry as a whole earns more and employs more, but each existing farmer’s price premium is competed away.
3 · It depends on. Whether Chinese demand keeps growing faster than new supply; competition from other exporters such as Vietnam and Malaysia; whether planting overshoots, so that many trees fruit at once and cause a glut, as happened with rubber; weather and disease; and which farmers we mean, since those who borrowed to plant late carry the risk while those with mature orchards banked the early gains.
4 · Judgement. For farmers already producing, the gain is greater in the short run, because inelastic supply turns the demand increase into a price increase on output they already have. In the long run the benefit is spread across more farmers and a larger industry, but per farmer it is smaller and less certain. The stronger argument is therefore that the short run benefits existing farmers more, while the long run benefits the industry, provided demand keeps growing.
Evaluation sentence starters: “In the short run… but in the long run…”, “This depends on whether…”, “This assumes that demand keeps rising, which may not hold because…”, “On balance, the stronger argument is… because…”
